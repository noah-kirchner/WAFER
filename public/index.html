About WAFER

WAFER (Wisconsin Analysis of Flood / Expansion Risk) identifies Wisconsin census tracts where new housing growth is colliding with rising, physically-measured flood risk — including tracts outside FEMA's official floodplain maps.

Methodology

Flood risk is measured from decades of USGS gauge history (how often a river has actually exceeded its flood stage, comparing the full historical record to the most recent 30 years), not from flood insurance claims — Wisconsin's ~1-2% NFIP take-up makes claims data reflect insurance-buying behavior, not actual flooding. Growth is measured from tract-level Decennial Census housing-unit change (2010→2020), not county-level, to avoid masking exactly where growth and risk overlap.

Priority Tiers

Every tract is scored on growth and nearby flood-risk trend alone — whether it's on FEMA's official floodplain map is tracked separately (see each tract's popup) rather than folded into this score, since it turns out most of water-dense Wisconsin intersects some mapped floodplain, which made "on the map or not" a weak first-cut signal on its own.

  • High Priority — real housing growth combined with a nearby river gauge showing a statistically rising flood-stage trend
  • Moderate Priority — real housing growth, but no nearby gauge currently shows a confirmed rising trend
  • Low Priority — little or no recent housing growth

Data Sources

  • USGS National Water Information System — historical peak-flow records
  • NOAA National Water Prediction Service — flood-stage thresholds
  • FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer — regulatory floodplain boundaries
  • U.S. Census Bureau — ACS 5-Year Estimates, Decennial Census, TIGERweb geometry
  • Wisconsin DHS Facilities (hospitals) and WI DPI (public schools)

Known Limitations

The 15km gauge-proximity buffer and the 0.10 trend-delta threshold are documented starting points, not validated findings. Not a live-monitoring tool — data refreshes periodically, which is the right cadence for a planning tool, not an emergency-response one.
